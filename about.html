About

I spent eight years making websites work. At some point I realized I didn’t want to spend the rest of my life changing the color of buttons.

My interest in biology was always there, but it was quiet. As a teenager, I watched ER, Grey’s Anatomy, and House, M.D., and loved the moments when someone dug into a problem nobody understood and found an answer that changed a patient’s life. At school, though, science didn’t click: chemistry a little, biology and physics not at all. Years later, I got curious about how the body actually works, and that curiosity kept pulling me deeper.

I thought about medicine, but years of training felt out of reach. Becoming a scientist wasn’t something people around me even considered: the pay was tiny, and you rarely heard what researchers were actually working on. I liked programming, so I built websites.

Now biology is becoming a data science, and that’s exactly where I want to be. Bioinformatics is my way in, then a master’s degree, with bioengineering as the goal.

Of course I’d love to change the world and cure every disease. But if I end up doing hard, meaningful work and doing it well, that will be more than enough.

This site is my open log: what I study each week, what I build, and where I get stuck.

Outside of the lab-to-be, I’m learning to run, which turns out to be just as humbling as learning biology.
